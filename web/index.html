  1. Where arrests happen. The campus is a grid of cells. Each cell's expected public cardiac arrests per year come from the people present by time of day, adult incidence (55 per 100,000 per year) and the extra risk of being out in public.
  2. How fast an AED arrives. A bystander walks to each candidate site and back, plus time to set off and to reach the AED inside the building. Locked buildings cannot help in that period. Only shockable-rhythm arrests (26.5%) benefit.
  3. Where to put them. An integer program (HiGHS) picks the sites that maximise expected survivors above what the ambulance alone would achieve, within the budget. Greedy is a fast near-optimal alternative; the Monte Carlo and sensitivity charts show how much the answer moves when the inputs are uncertain.

Team model: the team's illustrative 30-building campus with full results. York U (real): Keele campus buildings from OpenStreetMap; occupancy is modelled, not measured.
